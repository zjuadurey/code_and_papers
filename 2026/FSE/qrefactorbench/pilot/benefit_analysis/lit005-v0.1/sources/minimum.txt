  Finally, we prove theorem 1.
Proof of theorem 1: By lemma 3, after at most 2m0
iterations, T [y] holds the minimum value with proba-
bility at least 1/2. Assume for a moment that we run
the algorithm c times, each time for exactly 2m0 it-
erations. Let y1 , . . . , yc be the outcomes. Let y be yi
such that T [yi ] is minimum over all c outcomes. With
probability at least 1 − 1/2c , T [y] holds the minimum
value.
   The probability of success is even better if we run
the algorithm only once for exactly c2m0 iterations,
because then we use the information provided by the
previous iterations.
   If the values in T are not distinct, we use the
same algorithm as for the simplified case. The anal-
ysis of the general case is unchanged, except that
in lemma 2, the equation p(N, t) = 1/t now becomes
an inequality, p(N, t) ≤ 1/t. Hence, the lower bound
for the success probability given in theorem 1 for the
simplified case is also a lower bound for the general
case.
