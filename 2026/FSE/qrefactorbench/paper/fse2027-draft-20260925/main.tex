\title[Contract-Aware Quantum Opportunity Analysis]{Contract-Aware Quantum Opportunity Analysis: Enhancing LLMs with Program Analysis and Semantic Feedback}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous Author(s)}
\begin{abstract}
Identifying a quantum algorithm related to a classical computation is insufficient
to justify replacing that computation in existing software. A useful replacement
must respect input validation, exactness, ordering, exceptional behavior, and the
observable consequences of surrounding code. We study how program analysis and
semantic verification can strengthen a fixed large language model (LLM) in
identifying candidate computations and designing conditional quantum mappings.
QRefactorBench supplies source-traceable classical programs, controlled context
variants, explicit behavioral obligations, and scoped semantic checks for search
and combinatorial optimization. We formulate an evidence-producing workflow that
links candidate regions to program facts, represents mapping claims independently
of deployment recommendations, and returns reproducible counterexamples to the
same model. The evaluation design separates claim correctness from obligation
completion and compares direct prediction, self-review, analysis assistance, and
semantic feedback under recorded budgets. It further addresses context sensitivity,
checker validity, alternative encodings, transfer to unseen source lineages, and
the cost of classical fallback. This manuscript is a research draft: empirical
results are intentionally unfilled, and the proposed integrated method is not
presented as an already validated implementation. The intended contribution is
an auditable account of when classical analysis improves quantum mapping design,
without equating a plausible plan, a passing local check, or an executable circuit
with a correct migration or a quantum advantage.
\end{abstract}
